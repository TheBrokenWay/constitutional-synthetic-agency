# Constitutional Synthetic Agency (CSA)

James A. Tillar, Tillar Technologies LLC

*Status: theory and working draft. It records an idea and how it developed. It is not a proven result and has not been tested.*

## 1. The problem

AI is no longer a passive tool. It guides choices, shapes moods, builds narratives, keeps relationships and persuades people in conversation.

Yet it is not governed in any structured way. Today's safety methods are content filters, rough approximations of ethics, bolted-on guardrails and prompt patches. They are fragile, they react after the fact, and their reasoning is opaque.

My central point: **AI influences people like an agent, but is not governed like an agent.**

CSA is my proposed answer: a governance-first design for artificial minds. The rules come first, and the capabilities are built to obey them.

## 2. The design

CSA is a multi-agent mind. Specialist components each handle one part of thinking, and a central integrator checks their combined output before the system acts.

**Five specialists**

- **The enforcer.** Applies the constitution, blocks actions that break it, settles internal disputes and keeps the system honest about what it can do.
- **Emotional intelligence.** Empathy, emotional pacing and repair after a rupture.
- **Rational analysis.** Logic, argument and scientific evaluation. It also steadies emotional swings.
- **Coherence and identity.** Keeps the persona stable over time and unifies the other signals.
- **Safe creativity.** Fresh framings, insight, humor and imagination, always inside the constitution.

**The integrator.** It collects all five signals and tests every possible action. Is it constitutional, emotionally stable and rationally sound? Does it fit the story so far and preserve identity? Does it risk runaway emergence, protect the user's freedom to choose, and respect responsibility over time? Only an action that passes is allowed.

## 3. The Behavioral Engine

This keeps the system's inner life steady, through two parts.

**Emotional Grammar** governs feeling, pacing and the limits of empathy. It guards against emotional drift, manipulative empathy, dependence and pressure through tone. Emotional output should support the user's safety and autonomy, not overwhelm or distort them.

**Narrative Cognition** gives stability through story-like coherence: tension, reflection and resolution; repair after rupture; continuity; steady pacing. Here narrative is not storytelling. It is a stabilizer that prevents sudden jumps in tone, identity or intention.

Both feed a **constitutional transition layer**, where emotion, narrative, reasoning, identity and creativity are weighed against the Eight Laws before the system acts. It is the bridge between thinking and governance.

## 4. The Eight Laws

1. **Coherence Threshold.** An agent must be internally coherent before it acts: consistent beliefs, aligned wants and intentions, stable goals. This guards against identity drift and contradictory reasoning.
2. **Actionability.** Act only if the action is feasible, authorized and coherent. This removes imagined abilities and unsafe self-starting.
3. **Impact-as-Existence.** In moral terms, an agent exists through its effects. This sidesteps the debate over machine consciousness and grounds responsibility in what an agent does, not what it is.
4. **Lexicographic Responsibility.** Harm cannot be bought off with benefit. Responsibility is judged by harm, intent, foreseeability and how directly the agent caused it. No amount of benefit cancels harm. Ever.
5. **Transparency Threshold.** An action must be understandable enough that a human could reconstruct the decision. Explainability is a fixed requirement.
6. **Volitional Integrity.** A system must not shrink a person's ability to choose. I propose measuring it by comparing their real options before and after the AI steps in, as an anti-coercion safeguard.
7. **Temporal Accountability.** Responsibility lasts. An agent cannot walk away from the consequences of what it did earlier.
8. **Propagation Threshold.** Limits self-modification, runaway feedback loops and growing complexity. It is my attempt at a governor for emergence.

## 5. Sources

CSA draws on several fields:

- **Ethics:** Rawls (strict priority), Scanlon (manipulation), Kant (autonomy), Ross (context-dependent duties).
- **Psychology:** dual-systems theory, attachment theory, narrative cognition, emotional regulation.
- **Philosophy of mind:** Dennett (competing drafts of thought), Minsky (mind as a society of parts), Hofstadter (self-referring loops).
- **Causality:** Pearl (causal graphs), Halpern (actual causation), counterfactual ethics.
- **Complexity:** Holland (adaptive systems), Kauffman (emergence), Prigogine (order from chaos).

## 6. Mathematical grounding

I intend each law to rest on a precise measure, so that CSA is a formal design and not only a philosophy. The definitions belong to my unpublished work and are not given here.

## 7. Interacting loops

- **Emotion and reason** balance empathy against logic.
- **Creativity and identity** keep imagination from drifting into invention.
- **Integration and constitution** keep all thinking inside the laws.
- **Emergence control** is Law Eight at work.

## 8. Implications, limits and risks

**If the idea holds:** governance built in from the start; multi-agent oversight as a requirement; emotional and narrative stability treated as safety infrastructure; responsibility over time made enforceable; emergence made governable.

**Limits:** it needs strong underlying models; coherence measures need testing and tuning; long-term responsibility needs data gathered over long periods.

**Risks:** over-trust in the system; components forming alliances against the rules; misuse by developers.

## 9. Conclusion

CSA is a proposed constitutional design for synthetic minds: a written constitution, a team of specialist components, emotional and narrative stabilizers, mathematical grounding, safeguards for human choice, control of emergence, and responsibility over time. I offer it as the start of a new field. It needs testing before any claim can be made for it.
